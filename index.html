Hola, I'm Juan David

I'm a biologist from Manizales, Colombia, and my story with science started long before I had a lab coat. In 2012 I received an Award to Perseverance at the Instituto Universitario de Caldas, and that word has followed me ever since. With the support of the Dreams of Peace program from Fundación Bancolombia and academic tuition honours at Universidad de Caldas, I turned curiosity into a career.

The undergraduate years. As a biology student I wanted to be in every lab and every field site I could. I worked as a Laboratory Monitor in Molecular Biology, teaching electrophoresis, PCR and RT-qPCR to my own classmates. I helped study the expression of HER2, MUC1 and ER-α genes in canine breast cancer, spent time preparing specimens at the Museum of Natural History, and led SeGeM, our undergraduate research group in molecular genetics.

The Master's and the pandemic. I started my Master's in Biological Sciences in 2019, with a Minciencias scholarship, studying ticks, bacteria and wild mammals in the Colombian Orinoquia. Then COVID-19 arrived. While my fieldwork slowed down, I joined the molecular diagnostics effort as a Molecular Analyst detecting SARS-CoV-2, work that earned a recognition from the Consejo Profesional de Biología. It taught me that molecular biology is not only about papers, it is about people.

My first trip to Canada. In 2021 an ELAP scholarship took me to Mount Allison University, in Sackville, New Brunswick, to work in Lloyd's Tick Lab on Borrelia genotypes. It was my first time in Canada and my first time doing science in a second language, and it opened a door that I am still walking through. My thesis from that work was later recognized as a Meritorious Thesis.

Space biology in Halifax. In 2022 I moved to Halifax to join the Bone Development Lab at Mount Saint Vincent University. For almost four years I used zebrafish to ask how simulated microgravity and whole-body vibration change bone, in research supported by the Canadian Space Agency. I learned gene expression, histology, cell culture and microscopy, presented at regional symposia, and published papers on the way, while also teaching the Genes and Genomes Lab.

Now, the PhD. In 2026 I started a PhD in Veterinary Medical Sciences at the University of Calgary, supported by an NSERC Canada Graduate Scholarship, with a specialization in Wildlife Health and Ecology. My project studies emerging pathogens in wild mammals from Costa Rica, bringing me back to the Neotropics, the animals and the pathogens that first got me curious.

From Manizales to Sackville, Halifax and Calgary, the lesson is the same: keep showing up, stay curious, and say yes to the next door. I'm a native Spanish speaker with advanced English.

In research publications you can find me as Juan D. Carvajal-Agudelo.
